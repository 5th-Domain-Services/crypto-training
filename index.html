Lesson plan

Purpose and learning objectives

This lab builds practical cryptocurrency tracing skills with free, public blockchain tools. It works as a standalone exercise or alongside an instructor-led briefing. By the end, you should be able to:

  1. Profile an address: balance, transaction count, totals, and first funding source.
  2. Read a transaction's inputs, outputs, and fee, and test change-address assumptions.
  3. Identify Bitcoin address types and recognize invalid or mistyped addresses.
  4. Explain how blocks link and how transactions move from the mempool into a block.
  5. Use wallet clustering and service labels, and state their limitations.
  6. Separate payments made during a ransomware incident from later, unrelated deposits, using the WannaCry case.
  7. Visualize a fund flow and compare commercial attribution against native-chain data.
  8. Identify fiat off-ramps and write a report that separates observation from inference.

Recording time: always use UTC

Record every date and time in UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) so findings line up across tools, agencies, and time zones. Blockchains store block times as Unix timestamps in UTC, but many explorers convert them to your computer's local time.

  1. mempool.space: shows local time until you set the Time zone menu at the bottom of the page to "UTC - Greenwich Mean Time (GMT)".
  2. Blockchain.com: block pages show UTC (labeled). Transaction pages show local time, labeled "GMT" plus your offset, on a 12-hour clock with no AM or PM, so 7:08 PM appears as 07:08:21.
  3. WalletExplorer and MetaSleuth: show UTC without a label.
  4. Check any tool: look up block 0, which was mined at 2009-01-03 18:15:05 UTC. A different time means the tool is applying an offset.
  5. Check your computer: time.gov ↗ shows your local time next to UTC.
  6. Convert: timeanddate.com time zone converter ↗ and Epoch Converter ↗ (Unix timestamps).
